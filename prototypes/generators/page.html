New family · after Stoli's wall plotter

Sweeps: one line, drawn hundreds of times

Stoli's drawings get their depth from stacking, not from a cleverer formula. A short arc of fluid is carried through a swirling flow and drawn again every few moments. Hundreds of nearly identical curves read as a translucent veil: darker where they bunch up, with a dark crease and a crosshatch where the veil folds over itself. The flow here is a central vortex in turbulence (random waves with Kolmogorov's spectrum); in Folds and Satellites a breathing ring of satellite vortices folds the veils further.

The pen makes it possible

The previews add ink wherever lines cross, the way layered ballpoint lines darken on paper. Stoli draws with pressurised ballpoint refills (Uni PowerTank): they write on a vertical sheet, do not dry out uncapped, and take hundreds of passes without soaking the paper. A fineliner could not, which is why the six families below keep a 45 mm cap.

The ink cap is a placeholder

300 mm of line per 5 mm square is about four full coverings of a 0.35 mm line. It trims only the very densest spots. The pen test in Phase 1A sets the real figure for the chosen pen and paper.

Computation is part of the work

A sweep computes in seconds here and in minutes on a Pi 1, then draws for about an hour. Longer runs, more sheets and finer turbulence cost only time, and the frame can compute the next picture while the current one hangs.

The first six families

What the frame could draw by itself

All of them are lines a pen draws well: contour lines, streamlines, nodal lines and trajectories. Each picture is fully determined by its parameters and seed, so it can be redrawn exactly.

A roll as a film strip

One series: the cylinder starts to spin

A curator picks one family and one parameter sweep per roll. Here the circulation grows from zero, where the flow is symmetric, to 4πUa, where the two stagnation points meet under the cylinder. Gallery mode would replay the roll in this order, one picture every few hours.

What the prototype shows, and what's next

Shown

  • Every family fills the real frame; none needs rescaling afterwards.
  • Each picture computes in tens of milliseconds in a browser; on a Pi 1, about 30 times slower, that is still a couple of seconds against 5–25 minutes of drawing.
  • The ink limiter is real: it thinned the crowded slit region of the double slit and the fading centre of the harmonograph.

Not yet

  • The signature caption in a single-stroke plotter font.
  • Several colours, one layer per pen.
  • Retraject's own tool paths as a family.
  • A check for large empty areas, beyond the coverage number.

Next

  • Move the generators into the repo as TypeScript (src/generators/) with tests: every family stays inside the frame and the budget, and the same seed gives the same picture.
  • A generator page in the explorer, next to the others.
  • The pen test (Phase 1A): pressurised ballpoint on the chosen paper, to set the sweeps' ink cap.
  • Then the curator and the series player on the Pi (Phase 4).

Previews are computed by the same code that runs when you press a button: marching squares for the contour families, fourth-order Runge–Kutta for the Bohmian trajectories, and the Jobard–Lefer method for evenly spaced streamlines. The Bohmian trajectories use the exact free evolution of two Gaussian packets (ħ = m = 1). Sweeps carry every particle with fourth-order Runge–Kutta, add particles where neighbours drift more than 1 mm apart, and preview the ink with Beer–Lambert absorption: each layer of ink takes away a share of the light. Drawing times assume the plan's speeds and ignore acceleration, so real times run a little longer.
