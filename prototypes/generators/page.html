Six families

What the frame could draw by itself

All of them are lines a pen draws well: contour lines, streamlines, nodal lines and trajectories. Each picture is fully determined by its parameters and seed, so it can be redrawn exactly.

A roll as a film strip

One series: the cylinder starts to spin

A curator picks one family and one parameter sweep per roll. Here the circulation grows from zero, where the flow is symmetric, to 4πUa, where the two stagnation points meet under the cylinder. Gallery mode would replay the roll in this order, one picture every few hours.

What the prototype shows, and what's next

Shown

  • Every family fills the real frame; none needs rescaling afterwards.
  • Each picture computes in tens of milliseconds in a browser; on a Pi 1, about 30 times slower, that is still a couple of seconds against 5–25 minutes of drawing.
  • The ink limiter is real: it thinned the crowded slit region of the double slit and the fading centre of the harmonograph.

Not yet

  • The signature caption in a single-stroke plotter font.
  • Several colours, one layer per pen.
  • Retraject's own tool paths as a family.
  • A check for large empty areas, beyond the coverage number.

Next

  • Move the generators into the repo as TypeScript (src/generators/) with tests: every family stays inside the frame and the budget, and the same seed gives the same picture.
  • A generator page in the explorer, next to the others.
  • Then the curator and the series player on the Pi (Phase 4).

Previews are computed by the same code that runs when you press a button: marching squares for the contour families, fourth-order Runge–Kutta for the Bohmian trajectories, and the Jobard–Lefer method for evenly spaced streamlines. The Bohmian trajectories use the exact free evolution of two Gaussian packets (ħ = m = 1). Drawing times assume the plan's speeds and ignore acceleration, so real times run a little longer.
