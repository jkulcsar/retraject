retraject kinematics explorer

The R6 arm: six revolute joints described by Denavit–Hartenberg parameters, with a spherical wrist (joints 4–6 intersect in one point), which is what makes closed-form inverse kinematics possible. Drive it both ways: move the sliders (forward kinematics) or drag the gizmo (inverse kinematics — the analytic solver runs on every frame of the drag, picking the solution closest to the current pose, or pin one of the eight branches: shoulder front/back × elbow down/up × wrist flip). A red target means out of reach or outside joint limits — the robot holds, as a real controller would. Switch the gizmo to rotate to command orientation. Then program like a teach pendant: pose the robot (sliders or gizmo), add waypoint, pick a law per segment, play path — the multi-segment planner synchronizes all six joints per segment, the robot replays the motion, and the profile charts below scrub in time with the 3D scene. That loop — pose → plan → profiles → motion — is the 1997 thesis, whole.
